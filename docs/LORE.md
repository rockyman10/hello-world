# STARFALL: ECLIPSE PROTOCOL — Lore Bible
### Factions, rarity naming, character dossiers, and the Chapter 1 storyline

---

## 1. Rarity Naming — What We Call Legendaries

RAID uses Common → Mythical; Genshin/HSR use plain stars. We name tiers **in-world**, after the fidelity of a Voidborn's genome reconstruction — so rarity is lore, not just a label:

| Stars | Tier name | Lore meaning | Market equivalent |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3★ | **STRAND** | A partial genome strand — too unstable to anchor as a person; salvaged into Echo Shards | Rare / filler |
| 4★ | **VECTOR** | A complete, combat-stable reconstruction; the backbone of every squad | Epic |
| 5★ | **PRIME** | A perfect first-generation imprint of a pre-Eclipse life, anchored whole | Legendary |
| 6★ (future) | **SINGULARITY** | A Voidborn who has survived their own paradox — reserved for limited/mythic-tier releases | Mythical |

The pull screen says it out loud: *"Signal locked — PRIME reconstruction stable."* Players will say "I pulled a Prime" the way RAID players say "I pulled a lego."

## 2. The Five Genome Houses (the "space tribes")

Three centuries on the ark **Meridian** turned survivor fleets into cultures. Every Voidborn is built from one House's genome archive and raised in its deck-culture. Houses are **visual identity + character themes + squad mechanics** in one.

### ⚔ House Vantar — the Void Compact
The old explorer-aristocracy who signed the Void Compact: study the Eclipse from inside, whatever it costs. Umbral-affinity research is their monopoly; every Umbral unit in the game traces to Vantar vats. Deck aesthetic: black glass, thin gold light, silence. **Combat identity:** Umbral prestige units, HP-trading damage, executes. *Their sin: they knew the Eclipse was coming.*

### 📡 The Chorus — the Signal Saints
A human-AI media collective that kept the fleet sane through the dark centuries: idols, archivists, propagandists, signal-techs. Their broadcasts are literal battlefield tech — morale buffs, jamming arrays, shield-harmonic maps. Deck aesthetic: cassette-punk neon, shrine-studios, graffiti hymns. **Combat identity:** buffs, debuffs, turn-meter manipulation — the support house.

### ❄ Rimeholt Combine — the Hullbreakers
Salvager clans of the outer fleet who go into Eclipse-touched wrecks for what the ark needs to survive. Hard contracts, harder people; debts paid in salvage or blood. Deck aesthetic: frost-scarred plate, clan knots, trophy hull-plates. **Combat identity:** Cryo/Plasma bruisers, Corrosion damage-over-time, turn-meter drain — the attrition house.

### 🧬 Helix Gardens — the Gene-Artisans
The arcology that grows the Voidborn themselves — gardeners of the Genome Archive. Medics, splicers, philosophers; they treat every operative as a living argument that humanity can outgrow the Eclipse. Deck aesthetic: Afrofuturist biotech, living walls, gold-on-green. **Combat identity:** healing, cleansing, shields — the sustain house.

### 🛡 The Frameguard — the Last Standards
Knight-lineages who pilot ancestral combat frames handed down since Earth. Their oath is one sentence: *the line holds, or nothing does.* Deck aesthetic: heraldic mecha, oath-scrolls, ceremony. **Combat identity:** taunts, counterattacks, team plating — the wall house.

### ☄ The Eclipsed (enemy faction)
Not a House — what's left when the Eclipse rewrites one. Echo-copies of lost people, paradox drones, and the **Paradox Lords** who anchor rewritten timelines. Every enemy in the game belongs to the Eclipsed, which means every fight is, uncomfortably, against someone's ancestors.

## 3. Looping Factions Into Gameplay (not just flavor)

This is the answer to "easy to create factions, but we need more to loop it in" — each hook below gives factions a *mechanical* reason to matter:

1. **Kinship Protocol (in the prototype now):** field 2+ operatives of the same House and each gets **+12% ATK and +12% DEF**. Team-building question every battle: rainbow squad for affinity coverage, or House stack for stats?
2. **House Vaults (endgame, RAID Faction Wars model):** weekly dungeons that only allow one House's operatives. Instantly makes *depth* of collection matter, not just your best five — the single strongest driver RAID has for pulling niche units.
3. **House story arcs:** each chapter spotlights one House's culture and crisis; banner characters debut inside their House's arc so you meet them before you can pull them.
4. **Bond conversations by House:** advise-style character stories that unlock House lore; completing a House's bond set grants a permanent small Kinship upgrade for that House.
5. **House emblems (farming identity):** ascension materials are House-tagged, so farming routes have identity ("I need Chorus emblems this week").
6. **House events & rep tracks:** rotating events ("Rimeholt Salvage Season") with a free Vector of that House on the reward track — catch-up generosity with lore attached.

## 4. Character Dossiers

### Primes (5★)

**Kaelis Vantar — "The Unwritten Blade"** · House Vantar · Umbral/Attack
Heir of the Void Compact. When his home system fell, the Eclipse rewrote him into the enforcer of the Paradox Lord **Sable-of-Nine** — until the Navigator's anchor signal cut him loose mid-execution. Somewhere in a rewritten timeline, a version of him still serves. He intends to be the one who erases it.

**Juno-9 — "The Signal Saint"** · The Chorus · Ion/Support
An AI grown from the archived voice of a pre-Eclipse idol who died on Earth. Nine iterations of herself have burned out keeping the morale grid alive; this one has started writing her own songs, which the Chorus quietly considers a miracle. Her concerts are also jamming arrays.

**Solveig Rask — "Hullbreaker"** · Rimeholt Combine · Cryo/Attack
Third-generation salvager. Cut open a derelict at nineteen and found the echo-copies of her own clan inside, still working the wreck. Sealed the hull, logged the salvage, said nothing for six years. Pays her debts in corrosion now.

**Adaeze Okonkwo — "The Gardener"** · Helix Gardens · Plasma/Mender
Chief gene-artisan of the Gardens; half the Voidborn genome library is her handwriting. Refuses to call the operatives "assets" in official reports, which costs her a reprimand per report. Overheals on purpose: "growth beyond the wound is the whole point."

**Renji Kurosawa — "The Last Standard"** · The Frameguard · Cryo/Defense
Pilots the frame his family has maintained for eleven generations; its armor still carries a smear of Earth soil under lacquer. The last time a Kurosawa broke formation was never. He does not intend to update the record.

### Vectors (4★)

**Mika Tan — "The Static Cartographer"** · The Chorus · Ion/Support
Chorus signal-tech who maps enemy shield harmonics live on air — every Armor Breach she lands is, technically, a broadcast hit single. Free starter; the first friend every Navigator makes.

**Dex Volkov — "Cutter"** · Rimeholt Combine · Plasma/Attack
Plasma-torch specialist. Burns salvage loose and enemies looser. Owes Solveig either three life-debts or four; they've stopped counting in front of witnesses.

**Vex Marlowe — "The Storm Privateer"** · House Vantar · Ion/Attack
Runs contraband along the fracture lanes under a Vantar letter of marque. Insists the letter is genuine. The seal is upside down.

**Ora Chen — "Coldhands"** · Helix Gardens · Cryo/Mender
Cryo-surgeon. Keeps hearts beating at three kelvin and considers warmth a rumor. Trained under Adaeze; disagrees with her about everything except patients.

**Brick-7 — "The Door"** · The Frameguard · Ion/Defense
A decommissioned breach-frame that refused to power down and walked to the Frameguard chapterhouse to take the oath. Legally a door. The Frameguard's most beloved member.

## 5. Chapter 1 Storyline — "The Meridian Wakes" (in the prototype)

**Beat 1 — Perimeter Sweep.** The Navigator wakes from anchor-sleep to alarms: Eclipsed patrol drones on the hull. Mika, Dex, and Ora hold the line while the Navigator learns to anchor. In the drone wreckage: a Rimeholt distress beacon that shouldn't exist — its clan was logged lost thirty years ago.

**Beat 2 — The Cryo Cell.** The beacon leads to a salvage yard where the lost clan still "works" — echo-copies, rewritten by the Eclipse, all Cryo affinity, keeping a dead routine forever. Ora realizes the horror: the Eclipse doesn't kill; it *preserves wrongly*. A stabilized echo whispers one word before dissolving: the thing anchoring the rewrite. *Warden.*

**Beat 3 — The Paradox Warden.** At the heart of the yard, a Paradox Warden maintains the rewritten timeline, healing every wound reality inflicts on it. Sever its causal loop (Heal Blackout) or outrace its enrage. When it falls, the rewrite collapses, the clan's echoes finally rest — and something far away notices. A voice on a dead channel: *"You've unwritten my Warden, little Navigator. Kaelis. Come home."* — **Sable-of-Nine**, Chapter 2's antagonist and Kaelis's former master.

**Season arc sketch:** Ch. 2 — House Vantar's secret (they knew); Ch. 3 — the Chorus schism (a broadcast that predicts the Eclipse's next rewrite); Ch. 4 — Rimeholt's bargain (salvaging the Warden's core wakes something bigger). Each chapter debuts its House's banner Prime inside the story.
